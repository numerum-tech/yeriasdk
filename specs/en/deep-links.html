Description

A deep link opens your service — or one precise screen of it — from outside the app: a notification, an e-mail, a QR code, a message.

The link addresses your service, never your server. It carries the id Yeria gave your service, and the client resolves the rest: your base URL, your public keys, the country rule. That is what lets your links keep working when your infrastructure moves, and what keeps a forged link from pointing the app at a server that is not yours.

The two forms

Both say the same thing. Use whichever the medium allows.

FormUse
yeria://dl/{op}/{serviceId}Inside a notification, a QR code, an app that already knows Yeria is installed.
https://yeria.app/dl/{op}/{serviceId}Anywhere a web link is expected — an e-mail, a page, an SMS. It opens the app when it is installed.

Operations

opOpens
sYour service, at its base URL — its root view.
vOne of your views. Requires ?p=, see below.
cThe conversation between the user and your service.
pThe prompt to pin your service.
nThe prompt to consent to your notifications. This is the link to put behind a "get notified" call to action.

Linking to one of your views

v takes the path of the view on your own server, in a single p parameter:

yeria://dl/v/{serviceId}?p=/orders/4718
https://yeria.app/dl/v/{serviceId}?p=/orders/4718

A deep entry receives its root. The client mounts the view served by your base URL first, then places the requested one on top. The user therefore lands where you pointed them, and a back gesture leads into your service instead of out of the app. You have nothing to declare for this: it is how the client enters, always.

Build them with the SDK

YeriaLink writes these links for you, and refuses to write a broken one. A service id outside the accepted set, or a path that would escape your service, throws where you build it — rather than producing a string that silently opens nothing on someone's phone.

import { YeriaLink } from '@numerum-tech/yeriasdk';

YeriaLink.service('svc_4718');                       // your service
YeriaLink.component('svc_4718', '/orders/4718');     // one of your views
YeriaLink.chat('svc_4718');                          // the conversation
YeriaLink.pin('svc_4718');                           // the pin prompt
YeriaLink.subscribe('svc_4718');                     // the notification consent

// https://yeria.app/dl/s/svc_4718

HTTPS is the default, because it works on a web page as well as on a device with Yeria installed. Ask for the custom scheme where you already know it is:

YeriaLink.service('svc_4718', { format: 'yeria' });
// yeria://dl/s/svc_4718

YeriaLink.isValid(link) answers whether a link is one Yeria will honour — useful when a link reaches you from somewhere you do not control.

The Python SDK carries the same class, with the same five builders and the same validation.

What the client refuses

An external link is untrusted input. The client drops anything it cannot vouch for, silently — a refused link opens nothing rather than opening the wrong thing.

  • A host other than dl, or a domain other than yeria.app. Nothing else is a Yeria link.
  • A service id outside A-Z a-z 0-9 . _ ~ -, or longer than 128 characters.
  • Any query parameter other than the single p of a v link, or a fragment.
  • A p that is not a path on your server: an absolute URL, a protocol-relative one, a backslash, or a .. segment. A link may reach into your service; it may not reach out of it.
  • A port, or credentials in the URL.

Before the user is signed in

A link received while nobody is signed in is not lost: it is held, and honoured once the session is open. Your call to action works on a first install as it does on the tenth launch.

A service outside the user's country asks for confirmation before opening. If the user declines, nothing opens — plan for the case where your link does not lead where you expected.
